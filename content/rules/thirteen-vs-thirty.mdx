In -teen numbers, stress the last syllable and hold its ii twice as long as a short i. In -ty numbers, stress the first syllable and say the final i short, with a full t.

This works the same way in both styles, and it prevents mistakes with prices, dates and quantities.

## -teen and -ty numbers

<PairTable group="teen-ty" />

Twenty follows the -ty pattern: <Sp>**twen**-ti</Sp>, never <Sp>**twe**-ni</Sp>.

<Callout variant="note" title="Listening to native speakers">
  Before a noun, native speakers sometimes move the stress forward ('<Sp>**thəə**-tiin</Sp> days'), so the long ii and
  the final n are your most reliable signal. Americans may say thirty as <Sp>**thər**-di</Sp>: listen for the long ii
  instead.
</Callout>
