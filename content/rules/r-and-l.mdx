At the start of a syllable, make *r* and *l* with two different tongue positions: this deserves a few minutes of practice every day. English uses the difference to separate hundreds of words, while Kinyarwanda treats *r* and *l* as one sound. It works the same way in American and Standard English.

## Tongue positions

| Sound | Tongue | Lips | How to check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <Nowrap>*l* <Hear id="snd-l" label="l" /></Nowrap> | The tip presses on the ridge just behind your top teeth and stays there for the whole sound; the air flows out over the sides | In their normal position | You can hold the sound for 2 seconds (llll) without moving your tongue |
| <Nowrap>*r* <Hear id="snd-r" label="r" /></Nowrap> | Pull the tongue tip back until it is behind the ridge, pointing up, with a gap. The sides may rest against your upper back teeth | Pushed forward and slightly rounded, less than for w | Keep your tongue tip clear of the ridge and roof of your mouth. If the tip taps the ridge, that is the Kinyarwanda *r*, which English ears can hear as *l* or d |

<Callout variant="check" title="Warm up">
  Say la la la, then ra ra ra, then la ra la ra, 10 times each. On every *l* your tongue tip touches the ridge; on every *r* keep
  the tip clear of the ridge and roof of your mouth.
</Callout>
