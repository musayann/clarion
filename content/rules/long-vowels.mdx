Kinyarwanda already separates short and long vowels, and Standard English uses the same difference in many common pairs. Hold every long vowel (a double letter in the sound spellings) about twice as long as the short one, as you do in Kinyarwanda.

## can and can't

Say can't as kaant: aa held twice as long, then a full t. Say can as kan, or as an unstressed kən in the middle of a sentence.

<Callout variant="rule" title="Say 'cannot' when it matters">
  When the answer matters (a promise, an appointment, a request), say 'cannot' instead of can't.
</Callout>

When Americans speak, listen for stress: 'I kən <Sp>**go**</Sp>' means can, and 'I <Sp>**kant**</Sp> go' means can't.

## Short and long vowels

<PairTable group="short-long" />

The short i in ship sits between Kinyarwanda i and e; the short u in full sits between Kinyarwanda u and o.

<Callout variant="note" title="Always hold the ii">
  Always hold the ii in sheet, beach and piece: each one has a rude short-vowel partner.
</Callout>
