Your experience with short and long vowels gives you a useful starting point. Listen for both the length and the vowel sound: sheep needs more than a longer version of ship. A double letter in the sound spellings is a practice cue to hold the vowel longer and match the recording.

## *can* and *can't*

Say can't as kaant: hold the aa, match its sound to the recording, then say a full *t*. Say can as kan, or as an unstressed kən in the middle of a sentence. Listen for the change in vowel sound as well as length.

<Callout variant="rule" title="Say 'cannot' when it matters">
  When the answer matters (a promise, an appointment, a request), say 'cannot' instead of can't.
</Callout>

When Americans speak, listen for stress: 'I kən <Sp>**go**</Sp>' means can, and 'I <Sp>**kant**</Sp> go' means can't.
